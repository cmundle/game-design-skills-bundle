# Credits

These skills apply established frameworks from design, psychology and business
research to game design problems. The frameworks belong to their authors. What
is licensed in this repository is the skill instructions, structure and wording,
not the theory underneath.

## Player motivation and psychology

- **Player types** — Richard Bartle, *Hearts, Clubs, Diamonds, Spades: Players
  Who Suit MUDs* (1996). Used in `game-design-bartle-archetype-audit`.
- **Self-Determination Theory** — Edward L. Deci and Richard M. Ryan; applied to
  games as PENS by Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski (2006). Used in
  `game-design-player-need-satisfaction-audit`,
  `game-design-player-motivation-audit`,
  `game-design-granular-player-motivation-audit`.
- **Flow** — Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi, *Flow: The Psychology of Optimal
  Experience* (1990). Used in `game-design-flow-audit`.
- **Fogg Behavior Model** — BJ Fogg, Stanford Behavior Design Lab. Used in
  `game-design-fogg-behavior-audit`.
- **Big Five / OCEAN** — the five-factor model of personality, developed across
  the work of Tupes and Christal, Goldberg, Costa and McCrae. Used in
  `game-design-big-five-personality-audit`.
- **Attribution theory** — Bernard Weiner's model of locus, stability and
  controllability. Used in `game-design-attribution-audit`.
- **Empathizing-systemizing** — Simon Baron-Cohen. Used in
  `game-design-systematizing-empathizing-audit`.

## Cognition, memory and decision-making

- **Fast and slow thinking** — Daniel Kahneman, *Thinking, Fast and Slow*
  (2011). Used in `game-design-thinking-fast-and-slow-audit`.
- **Peak-end rule** — Daniel Kahneman and colleagues. Used in
  `game-design-peak-end-audit`.
- **Zeigarnik effect** — Bluma Zeigarnik (1927). Used in
  `game-design-zeigarnik-effect-audit`.
- **Gambler's fallacy and related misperceptions of randomness** — Amos Tversky
  and Daniel Kahneman. Used in `game-design-perceived-randomness-audit`.
- **Kano model** — Noriaki Kano. Informs
  `game-design-social-satisfaction-matrix`.

## Narrative and creative method

- **The Hero's Journey / monomyth** — Joseph Campbell, *The Hero with a Thousand
  Faces* (1949), and Christopher Vogler's screenwriting adaptation. Used in
  `game-design-ftue-hero-journey-audit`.
- **SCAMPER** — Bob Eberle, after Alex Osborn. **Six Thinking Hats** — Edward de
  Bono. **Brainwriting 6-3-5** — Bernd Rohrbach. **How Might We** — developed at
  Procter & Gamble and popularised by IDEO. **Crazy 8s** — Google Ventures
  design sprint. **Lotus Blossom** — Yasuo Matsumura. **Morphological analysis**
  — Fritz Zwicky. All used in `game-design-brainstorm-methods`.
- **Oblique Strategies** — Brian Eno and Peter Schmidt (1975), as a method
  influence on `game-design-creative-unblocker`.

## Process and management

- **GROW model** — Graham Alexander, Alan Fine and Sir John Whitmore. Used in
  `game-design-grow-design`.
- **SMART goals** — commonly credited to George T. Doran (1981). Used in
  `game-design-smart-goal-audit`.
- **Pre-mortem** — Gary Klein. Used in `design-red-team-audit`.
- **Backcasting** — John B. Robinson. Used in
  `game-design-ideal-outcome-backcasting`.
- **Known and unknown unknowns** — as a risk-framing convention in project
  management. Used in `game-design-unknown-unknowns-prototyping`.

## Named lenses

- **The Molyneux lens** in `game-design-molyneux-lens` is a deliberately
  provocative design posture named after Peter Molyneux's public reputation for
  ambitious overstatement. It is a critique method, not an endorsement,
  affiliation, or a claim about him personally.

## A note on scope

Where a skill names a framework, it applies that framework's logic in this
author's own words and structure. No skill reproduces the text of any book,
paper or copyrighted instrument. Readers who want the source material should go
to the originals, which are better than any summary of them.
